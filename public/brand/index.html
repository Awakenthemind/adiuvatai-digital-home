Brand Brief

BRAND BRIEF

What we sell. Who we sell to. What we believe.

Business

What we sell: Creative consulting — brand strategy, design, campaigns, and execution for businesses that need serious work done without the agency theater. We're not a retainer factory. We're a weapon in your arsenal.

Tagline: The Work Behind The Work.

Customer

The person who hires us: CEO, founder, or brand/creative director at a company that's past the startup phase but not yet a household name. They have real revenue ($1M–$50M), real problems, and they're tired of agencies that show up with a deck and disappear.

They're tactical. They want someone embedded, not a consultant who visits. They've been burned by agencies that overpromise, underdeliver, and charge like they did both. They want precision. They want speed. They want someone who understands the game.

What they want: Someone who handles the work so they can focus on what only they can do.

What they hate: Agencies that speak in jargon, waste time in discovery, and deliver specs instead of results. Bloat. Theater. The "we need to sync" culture.

Primary CTA

When someone is ready to work: Get in touch. No formal "request a demo." Just a conversation.

Strong Opinion / Wedge

Most agencies sell the illusion of creative excellence. We sell execution. The work is the strategy — not the slides, the mood boards, or the 12-point positioning framework that sits in a folder after the kickoff meeting. If the output doesn't move the needle, the strategy failed.

We'd rather ship one lethal campaign than produce a beautiful deck that nobody remembers.

Voice Traits

Authoritative. Precise. Unflinching. Clinical. Lethal elegance.

5-word self-description: Sharp, embedded, relentless, surgical, clean.

Words we use: precise, structural, embedded, relentless, surgical, lethal, unflinching, tactical, armor, weapon, bullet.

Words we don't: synergy, leverage, best-in-class, cutting-edge, robust, holistic, innovative, forward-thinking.

Ideal Customer Profile

ICP

Who we work with. Who we don't. How to recognize the right fit.

The Person

CEO, founder, brand director, or creative director at a company with:

  • Revenue: $1M–$50M (past startup phase, not yet household name)
  • Team: Has a team but needs outside firepower for specific problems
  • Problem: Something is broken — positioning, brand, campaign, execution — and they need it solved, not discussed

They don't have time to manage an agency. They need a partner who handles the work while they handle the rest.

Psychographic Profile

What they want:

  • Someone who gets in the arena with them, not above it
  • Work that moves the needle, not metrics that look good in a report
  • Clarity. Speed. Precision.
  • Someone who says what they see and does the work.

What they hate:

  • Agency theater — the jargon, the decks, the "let's circle back"
  • Retainer culture — paying for time instead of buying outcomes
  • Being sold to before being trusted
  • The 30-page strategy doc that gets bookmarked and forgotten

What they're tired of:

  • Agencies that show up with expertise and disappear after the pitch
  • Being managed instead of supported
  • The gap between what was promised and what was delivered

Decision Triggers

They reach out when:

  • A launch is imminent and the current plan is falling apart
  • They've been burned by an agency and need someone who won't repeat the mistake
  • They have a clear problem and need it solved — not a discovery phase
  • The internal team is maxed out and they need firepower
  • They need someone embedded, not a vendor they manage

What They're NOT

  • Early-stage startups looking for "brand identity" from scratch
  • Companies that want the cheapest option
  • People looking for a vendor, not a partner
  • Anyone who needs their hand held through every decision

ICP Tiering

Tier 1 — Primary

Profile: Founder or CEO, $3M–$20M revenue, has been through the agency wringer at least once. Clear problem, clear budget, clear timeline. They want a weapon, not a workshop.

What they need: Execution. Speed. A partner who doesn't need managing.

Tier 2 — Secondary

Profile: Brand or creative director, mid-sized team, running point on a specific initiative. Has budget authority but needs internal buy-in. They want someone who makes them look good.

What they need: Precision work that they can defend to leadership. Clarity. No theater.

Tier 3 — Opportunistic

Profile: Marketing director at a company doing a rebrand or launching a new product line. Budget and deadline, not enough internal resource.

What they need: To feel confident that they're hiring the right team. Proof that we can handle their specific situation.

ICP Signal Phrases

Watch for these in initial conversations:

  • "We've done the agency thing before and it wasn't..."
  • "We need someone who can just handle it"
  • "The last team was great on paper but..."
  • "We don't have time to manage another agency"
  • "We need this done in [X] weeks, not [X] months"

Markets We Serve Best

  • Professional services (law firms, consultancies, financial advisors)
  • Tech companies past Series A that need positioning clarity
  • Consumer brands with distribution but no brand coherence
  • B2B companies that need to speak human

Voice & Tone

VOICE

"Say less. Mean more."

The Voice

We sound like someone who says less and means more. We don't pad. We don't hedge. We don't perform expertise — we demonstrate it.

Core characteristics:

  • Direct — Say less. Mean more.
  • Precise — Every word has a job. None of them are wasted.
  • Unflinching — We don't soften feedback to be liked.
  • Cold — Not unfriendly. Just not warm for the sake of it.
  • Surgical — Find the problem fast. Solve it cleaner.

Tone by Context

ContextTone
Brand voice (public)Authoritative, sharp, confident. Lead with the punch.
Client communicationDirect, precise, no-nonsense. Clear next steps.
ProposalsClinical, structural, proof-heavy. Show, don't tell.
Internal (wiki/notes)Terse. Notes, not prose. Use shorthand.
Social / contentConfident, provocative where it serves. No hedging.

Syntax Rules

  • Sentence length: Short to medium. No compound paragraphs.
  • Punctuation: Commas for breath. Periods for impact.
  • Numbers: Digits — 3 weeks, $50K, 30%
  • ALL CAPS: Only for critical warnings, brand moments, display headlines. Not for emphasis.
  • Contractions: Always. "Don't" not "do not."
  • First person: "We" for Adiuvatai. "You" for the client. Never "one."

Words We Use

precise, surgical, structural, embedded, relentless, lethal, clean, sharp, cold, direct, fast, done, execution

Words We Don't Use

leverage, synergy, best-in-class, cutting-edge, robust, holistic, innovative, forward-thinking, game-changer, disrupt, ecosystem, value-add, pivot, circle back, sync, deep dive, agile, scalable, solution, methodology

Leading With The Punch

Rule: Never bury the lead. The first line should be the point.

"We believe that in today's competitive landscape, brand positioning is more important than ever, which is why we focus on..."

"Your brand isn't a logo. It's the work you do when no one's watching. We make sure it counts."

What We Never Sound Like

  • A LinkedIn thought leader
  • An agency pitch deck
  • A consultant who needs to explain why they're worth it
  • Someone who's still figuring it out

Sells time, not outcomes. Builds decks, not campaigns. Disappears after the pitch. Charges for process, not proof.

Inconsistent quality. No structural backup. Limited capacity. No accountability ecosystem.

We're neither. We sell execution. We embed. We deliver.

Traditional Agencies

What they do: Credentials, case studies, resources, scale

What they screw up: Speed, context, honesty, execution

Our angle: No overhead. No process theater. We have the work.

Boutiques and Small Studios

Our angle: Structure. Backup. Capacity without sacrificing quality.

Content Strategy

CONTENT

What we publish. Why. How.

Why We Create Content

We're not building a content empire. We're building proof. Every piece we publish should demonstrate one of two things:

  • We know the game — strategic insight, industry observation, the truth others won't say
  • We can do the work — case studies, process breakdowns, the output itself

Content Pillars

01

Brand Truth

Observations and convictions about brand, positioning, and the work — stated clearly, without hedging. Show we see the game clearly.

Short-form articles, LinkedIn posts. Themes: "Why most brand guidelines are useless", "Your brand isn't your logo", "Why 'synergy' is a red flag".

02

Process Proof

How we do what we do — the actual methodology, not the pitch version. Show the machinery. We have systems, not just taste.

Framework docs, process breakdowns. Themes: "How we run a brand sprint", "The intake conversation", "How we structure a campaign".

03

Client Wins

Proof — anonymized or with permission, specific, real numbers. Build credibility without self-promotion. Let the work speak.

Case study summaries, pull quotes, before/afters. Themes: "How we helped [company] solve [problem]", "The brief vs. what we delivered".

04

Industry Call-Outs

What we see wrong in the market, said plainly. Demonstrate the unflinching voice. Not here to be liked — here to be right.

Short posts, opinion pieces. Themes: "Why most agencies fail at execution", "The retainer model is broken", "Stop hiring agencies to manage".

Content Rules

  • No fluff. Every piece has a point. Make it.
  • Proof over promise. Show, don't tell.
  • Plain speech. No jargon.
  • One idea per piece. Not three loosely related observations.
  • Lead with the punch. First line is the point.
  • Be useful. If someone reads this, they should get something concrete.

Format Guidelines

Methodology

FRAMEWORKS

Proprietary systems built from doing the work.

This section holds the methodology docs — the structured approaches we use to solve problems. Not theories. Not frameworks we found in a book. Systems we've built from doing the work.

Emerging Frameworks

Frameworks to be documented as they emerge from actual client work:

  • Brand clarity methodology (from Brand Sprint)
  • Campaign execution process
  • Embedded partner workflow
  • Brand wiki ingestion process

Framework Template

When documenting a new framework, use this structure:

What it's for: The problem this solves. The context it's used in.

How it works: Step-by-step. Not theoretical steps — the actual steps we take.

When to use it: The specific situations where this framework applies.

What it outputs: The deliverable or decision that comes out the other side.

Key decisions: The places where judgment matters, not just process.

Notes: Edge cases, common mistakes, what to watch for.
